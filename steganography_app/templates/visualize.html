Understanding how different steganography methods work across various media types

Least Significant Bit (LSB)

LSB steganography works by replacing the least significant bits of data with message bits. This technique is simple but effective for various media types.

Image LSB

In images, LSB modifies the last bit of pixel values, causing minimal visual change while storing data.

Audio LSB

For audio files, LSB modifies the least significant bits of audio samples, maintaining audio quality.

Text LSB

Text is first converted to binary, then hidden using LSB in the carrier file.

Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)

DCT transforms data into frequency components, allowing data hiding in frequency coefficients.

Image DCT

Images are divided into 8x8 blocks, transformed to frequency domain, and coefficients are modified to hide data.

Audio DCT

Audio signals are transformed to frequency domain where data is hidden in DCT coefficients.

Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT)

DWT decomposes data into different frequency sub-bands, providing multiple locations for data hiding.

Image DWT

Images are decomposed into frequency sub-bands (LL, LH, HL, HH), with data typically hidden in detail coefficients.

Audio DWT

Audio signals are decomposed into approximation and detail coefficients using wavelets.
